## What the Deified Full Release Typically Changes

Full releases rarely rewrite a game from scratch. What they usually do is tighten the numbers that early access left loose: loot tables, experience curves, enemy scaling, and skill tuning. That tightening is exactly what puts your existing progression at risk.

Community reports from comparable action-RPG launches suggest a common pattern. Account-level and cosmetic rewards tend to be preserved, because wiping them upsets the most loyal players. Balance-sensitive values — character level curves, gear affixes, difficulty ladders — are far more likely to be recalculated, because the developers finally have enough data to fix what was broken.

## How Progression Works in Deified: The Core Tracks

Most progression-driven action games, Deified included, run several parallel tracks at once. Confusing them is the most common reason players feel stuck. Each track resets on a different schedule, rewards a different currency, and matters at a different point in your run.

| Track | What it governs | Reset risk at launch | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Character level | Base stats, skill access | Medium | High |
| Skill tree | Build identity, active abilities | Medium to high | High |
| Gear and crafting | Damage, defense, affixes | Low to medium | Medium |
| Meta-progression | Permanent account bonuses | Low | Very high |
| Difficulty tiers | Enemy scaling, reward multipliers | High for ladders | Medium |
| Collection and codex | Lore entries, cosmetics, records | Very low | Low |

A few practical notes on each:

- **Character level** is the most visible number and the least important long term. It gates content, but it rarely defines your power ceiling once gear and skills come online.
- **The skill tree** is where your build actually lives. If the deified full release reworks nodes, your muscle memory will be wrong for a few hours — plan for that.
- **Meta-progression** is the quiet winner. Bonuses that apply account-wide almost always survive a launch, which makes them the safest investment of your final pre-launch hours.
- **Difficulty tiers** are usually the first thing wiped, because leaderboards and ladders need a clean slate to mean anything.

## Preparing Your Save Before the Deified Full Release

Preparation is not about hoarding. It is about reducing the amount of re-farming you will have to do on launch day. Work through this checklist in order; the first two items take minutes and save hours.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Back up your save file locally | Protects you if a migration script fails |
| 2 | Screenshot every build and loadout | Lets you rebuild fast if trees are reworked |
| 3 | Bank crafting materials, not finished gear | Materials usually convert; gear often does not |
| 4 | Finish near-complete account unlocks | These tend to persist and give permanent value |
| 5 | Clear easy achievements and codex entries | Low effort, low reset risk |
| 6 | Read the launch patch notes fully | The only source that overrides guesswork |

A word on timing: player experience suggests that the final week before a launch is the worst time to start a long grind. If a task cannot be finished before the deified full release, it is usually better to postpone it until the new progression curve is live — the post-launch curve is often more generous, because developers want new players to catch up quickly.

## Progression Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced players fall into the same traps during a launch window. Avoiding these five mistakes will put you ahead of most of the player base.

| Mistake | What it costs you | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Grinding to a level cap that moves | Hours of wasted effort | Stop at the soft cap, bank materials |
| Chasing a build that gets reworked | Rebuild time and frustration | Keep two flexible loadouts ready |
| Ignoring meta-progression | Permanent bonuses missed | Clear account milestones first |
| Starting on the hardest tier | Slower progression, more deaths | Clear the new baseline, then push |

Two of these deserve extra emphasis. First, the level cap. If the deified full release raises the cap — and most launches do — any experience you banked above the old ceiling is often lost or heavily discounted. Second, difficulty. New tiers almost always arrive with a fresh reward multiplier, and pushing into them before you understand reworked mechanics is the fastest way to stall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will my early-access progress carry into the deified full release?**
It depends on the system. Account-wide unlocks and cosmetics are usually preserved, while difficulty ladders and some balance-sensitive values are reset. Check the official patch notes for the definitive answer rather than relying on secondhand posts.

**Should I keep playing right up until the deified full release launches?**
Play if you enjoy it, but do not start long grinds you cannot finish. Short, high-value tasks — backing up your save, finishing near-complete account unlocks, documenting your build — are the best use of your remaining time.

**How long does it take to reach endgame progression after launch?**
That varies by player skill and by how the new experience curve is tuned. Most players find the post-launch curve faster than early access, since developers want newcomers to catch up. Plan for a shorter ramp, not a longer one.

**Where can I verify progression details before I commit?**
The developer's official storefront page and patch notes are the only reliable sources. Community wikis and forums are useful for build ideas, but treat any pre-launch progression claim as unconfirmed until the studio publishes it.
