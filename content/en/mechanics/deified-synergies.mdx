Every build in a deification system looks unstoppable on paper — until you realize that raw power alone rarely carries a run. **Deified synergies** are what turn a scattered pile of divine boons into a machine that scales faster than the content around it. If you have ever stacked two blessings that each felt great alone but did nothing together, you already know why this matters.

This guide breaks down how deified synergies are built, which stacking rules decide whether your damage, healing, or defense actually multiplies, and how to test a combination before you commit an entire run to it. Whether you are chasing a first clear or pushing a late-game ceiling, the same principles apply.

## What "Deified Synergies" Actually Mean

When a character ascends, the game usually stops treating them as a mortal with upgrades and starts treating them as a new ruleset. You get divine resource types, new damage tags, new trigger conditions, and new ways to convert one stat into another. Synergy is the layer that wires those rules together.

Three questions separate a real synergy from a coincidence:

- **Shared tags.** Do the pieces reference the same keyword, element, or damage type? Tag overlap is the cheapest form of synergy because it costs nothing to activate.
- **Feeding loops.** Does piece A generate the resource that piece B spends? Loops are what let a build "go infinite" in the mid-game.
- **Conversion.** Does one effect turn a stat you already have into a stat you actually need? Conversion is usually the strongest — and the most fragile — form.

If a combination answers none of those questions, it is not a synergy. It is two good items sitting next to each other.

## The Five Families of Deified Synergies

Almost every ascension system, regardless of theme, sorts its powers into a handful of functional families. Knowing which family a boon belongs to tells you what it wants to be paired with — before you ever read a wiki entry.

| Family | What it does | Signature behavior | Pairs best with | Common trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amplifiers | Increases the value of an effect you already have | Multiplies an existing number rather than adding a new one | Triggers | Useless if you have nothing to amplify |
| Triggers | Converts one action into another effect | On-hit, on-kill, on-cast style chains | Amplifiers | Chains can stall if the trigger never fires |
| Converters | Turns one stat or resource into another | Defense into damage, health into mana, etc. | Sustain | Double conversion can zero out your own output |
| Sustain | Keeps you alive long enough for the loop to spin | Shields, lifesteal, regeneration, cleanse | Amplifiers | Over-investing stalls your scaling |
| Tempo & economy | Shortens the gap between loops | Cooldown reduction, extra casts, resource refunds | Everything | Feels weak early, wins late |

A healthy deified build usually touches at least three families. A build that touches only one is a stat-stick — it works until the difficulty curve outruns it.

## How Stacking Rules Decide Whether You Actually Scale

Two players can run the same three boons and get wildly different results, because stacking rules decide the order of operations. This is where most deified synergies quietly fail.

### Additive versus multiplicative

Additive bonuses add flat values together before any multiplier touches them. Multiplicative bonuses compound. A handful of multipliers will always beat a large pile of additive sources once your base numbers are high enough — which is why "more of the same" stops working around the mid-game.

### Thresholds and diminishing returns

Many systems also apply soft caps. Below the threshold, every point counts. Above it, returns flatten hard. Community reports across ascension-style games consistently describe the same pattern: the players who break runs are the ones who push *just* to a threshold and then reinvest elsewhere.

| Stack type | How it combines | Strongest when | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Additive | Flat values summed, then multiplied | Early game, low base stats | Falls off once multipliers arrive |
| Multiplicative | Compounds with each other | Mid-to-late game | Needs a stable base to multiply |
| Trigger/proc | Fires repeatedly during combat | You have high action frequency | Dead if the trigger condition stalls |
| Conversion | Rewrites one stat into another | Your build overflows a single stat | Self-defeating if over-converted |
| Threshold | Unlocks a bonus past a cutoff | You can hit the cutoff reliably | Wasted investment just short of the cap |

## Five Deified Synergy Patterns Worth Building Around

Patterns are reusable templates. You can drop different boons into the same pattern and get a working build, which makes them far more useful than memorizing individual combos.

| Pattern | Core requirement | Payoff | Investment | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chain Reaction | One reliable trigger plus one amplifier | Damage scales with enemy density | Medium | Weak against single targets |
| Single-Stat Overload | Stack one stat, then convert the overflow | Extremely high burst | High | One bad conversion bricks it |
| Conversion Loop | Two converters that feed each other | Self-sustaining resource engine | High | Prone to over-conversion |
| Passive Fortress | Sustain plus retaliation or reflect effects | Wins long fights automatically | Low | Slow against timers |
| Companion Web | Summons plus effects that scale off ally count | Multiplies total actions per second | Medium | Fragile if summons die instantly |

Player experience suggests the Conversion Loop is the highest ceiling and the lowest floor: when it comes online it trivializes encounters, and when it does not, you are holding three dead boons.

## Common Mistakes That Break Deified Synergies

Most failed runs are not caused by bad luck. They are caused by the same handful of structural errors.

- **Chasing rarity over fit.** A rare boon that shares no tags with your build is worse than a common one that completes a loop.
- **Stacking the same family twice.** Two amplifiers with nothing to amplify produce nothing.
- **Ignoring action frequency.** Trigger-based deified synergies need you to act often; slow, heavy builds starve them.
- **Over-converting.** Converting too much of a defensive stat can leave you with no survivability once the fight drags.
- **Committing before testing.** Pivoting late is expensive; verifying early is nearly free.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rarity-first drafting | Breaks tag overlap and loop timing | Draft by role, not by color |
| Duplicate families | No multiplier, no payoff | Track your families as you pick |
| Slow trigger rate | Procs rarely fire | Add tempo or attack-speed sources |
| No defensive floor | You die before scaling | Reserve one sustain slot |
| Late pivots | Wasted upgrades and rerolls | Test the core loop in the first act |

## How to Test and Optimize a Synergy Build

Testing is a skill, and it is the fastest way to improve. Rather than running a full clear to see whether a combination works, isolate the loop and stress it early.

| Step | What to do | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Identify the core | Name your two-piece engine out loud | If you cannot name it, you do not have one |
| 2. Check tags | Confirm shared keywords or elements | Overlap should be obvious, not hopeful |
| 3. Stress the trigger | Fight a dense group and a single elite | Both should still fire your loop |
| 4. Watch the resource | Track whether the engine refills itself | A loop that stalls is not a loop |
| 5. Add one amplifier | Insert a single multiplier | Damage or sustain should jump visibly |
| 6. Cap and reinvest | Stop at the soft cap, spend elsewhere | Gains should stay efficient |

Two more practical habits help. First, keep a short written note of the three boons that carried your last run — patterns repeat more than individual picks do. Second, when you find a combination that works, deliberately push it into content that is slightly too hard. Deified synergies reveal their real ceiling only under pressure, and the failures are more informative than the wins.

## FAQ

**Do deified synergies matter more than raw stats?**
Almost always. Raw stats give you a higher starting point, but deified synergies determine whether that point grows. A build with two multipliers and modest stats will overtake a build with huge stats and no multipliers once fights get long enough.

**How many boons should a core synergy use?**
Two or three. A two-piece engine that activates reliably beats a five-piece combo that only assembles once every ten runs. Add amplifiers only after the core loop is stable.

**Why does my synergy stop working in the late game?**
Usually one of three reasons: you hit a soft cap and kept investing, your trigger frequency fell behind enemy health pools, or you converted away the stat that kept you alive. Check the stacking table above and rebalance rather than adding more of the same.

**Can I force a specific deified synergy every run?**
Rarely with full reliability, which is exactly why patterns are more valuable than specific combos. Learn the shape of the loop you want, then accept whichever boons fill those slots. Players who draft by role instead of by name report far more consistent clears.
